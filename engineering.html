Force Feedback Steering Wheel

This is a custom Force Feedback Steering Wheel that uses a repurposed hoverboard motor for torque. A force feedback steering wheel allows me to input mechanical turning data into the computer with realistic feedback, as the (hoverboard) motor can use dynamic torque to change the force to turn the steering wheel which can physically simulate elements such as driving on different road surfaces, turning at different speeds, and more. With that, I've used FFBeast software to analyze the inputs and connect it to a driving/racing game of my choice. It features 12 3D printed PLA plastic parts designed in SolidWorks, a USB button board, a Servo Motor Controller, a Rotary Encoder, and a 24V power supply. Through this project, I've learned about compatible electronics, wiring electronics, a lot about mechanical fastening, how to solder effectively, how to better CAD in SolidWorks, and root cause analysis. Regenerative Braking Benchtop Rig

To go inline with my previous experiences related to automotive systems, I thought creating a model regenerative braking system would be a great learning experience for me. One of my goals for this project was to strengthen my knowledge of Arduinos and incorporate MATLAB for simulation and hardware control in which I formerly was only familiar with using the Arduino IDE for this purpose. This project was a great place to practice what I learned in Simulink/Simscape and also becoming more familiar with the transition from a simulation environment to a physical rendition. Its important to note that the use of Simscape is how I figured out a diode would be necessary in this circuit, it would be much harder to diagnose the cause of capacitor leakage without all the digital tools avaliable.

Individual Project

Duck Childrens Toy

A team of three and I were tasked with creating a children's toy for our final project in our engineering seminar class. The highlighting restriction for this design was that it had to follow appropriate universal design principles. This meant our team had to consider aspects such as ease of use, safety, and accessibility for children of different ages and abilities. Our idea was to create a waterproof Duck Toy that could move in water by spinning its legs. My individual task was to help design, model, print, and assemble the outer body of the duck. Using OnShape, the model uses large body pieces which maximized interior space for the electronic components and made it safer for children to handle. A smaller amount of total body peices also allowed for a better water seal. This project was one of my first experiences with 3D printing. With that, I learned how to better design components with 3D printing in mind, how to troubleshoot 3D printing issues, and how to better tune 3D printer settings such as printing patterns, infill, and material type for my specific application.

Group Project

SolidWorks Go-Kart Rolling Chassis

A group of four peers and I were required to model something of choice in SolidWorks 2025 that contained at least 25 parts, utilized gear/mate systems, and a motion study. My group decided that we would create an accurate go-kart rolling chassis. With that, our focus was not to model the inside of the engine and instead focus on modeling every other part with accuracy. We split the project into chassis, steering system, braking system, and body. I was responsible for the braking system, body panels, and part of the chassis. I was able to apply a lot of my previous knowledge from how automotive systems work, However this did not work for all parts of every system and I was still able to learn a lot about how go karts work. One of the major challenges I faced during this project was transferring my previous knowledge of automotive systems into a CAD environment. However this turned into a learning experience where I'm now much more experienced with translating real life component movements into a combination of rigid bodies, mates, and motion studies.
